# Email 5 — Day 18 — The Direct Ask

## Subject line options

- A: The actual reason I'm emailing you
- B: I want to put your name in the book
- C: 15 minutes, sync or async

## Preview text

Why your voice in particular.

## Body

A confession, then an ask.

I've been emailing you about your quiz result because I'm writing a book about how Christian men should hold the AI question, and I'm building a tool alongside it. There's no course at the end of this sequence. The only honest way to write the book is to talk to Christian men about it, one at a time. That's what these interview asks have actually been about the whole time.

15 to 30 minutes. Your quiz result on the screen between us. We walk through it together. I ask questions, you answer the ones you want to.

The format is whatever fits your week. A sync call on video or phone where we pick a time. Or async, where you record a voice memo or write replies on your own schedule and I send the questions over.

What I'm listening for is the real stuff. The thoughts still half-formed, and the places where your conviction and your daily practice haven't lined up yet.

If you say yes, your story shapes the book. With your name attached or anonymous, your call.

If you say no, the weekly prompt keeps showing up either way.

If you say yes, and 15 minutes feels doable, pick a slot here:

[calendly.com/discipleai](https://calendly.com/discipleai)

Or hit reply with the word "async" and I'll send you the questions to do on your time.

Thank you for taking the quiz. Thank you for reading this far.

Mitch

**P.S.** The men who've said yes so far have, almost without exception, told me afterward that the conversation itself was useful to them. That surprised me at first. It shouldn't have. There aren't many places to think out loud about this, and I'm trying to make one.
